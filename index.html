Dr. Samuel Loibl

His research to date has focused on insolvency, insurance and (comparative) tort law. He works extensively with artificial intelligence, particularly large language models, developing software and workflows for legal research and scholarly writing, with an emphasis on model evaluation, legal reasoning and source verification.

Personal projects

RIS-Direkt-Suche

A browser extension for searching Austria’s legal information system (RIS) from the address bar. It also turns recognised legal references on web pages and in PDFs into direct links to legislation and court decisions.

Project & installation/Source code

WordBridge

An MCP bridge connecting Claude Code / Codex to Microsoft Word with tracked changes. It stores research notes, decisions and workflow state across sessions, supporting autonomous cycles of research, synthesis and legal reasoning by multiple agents coordinated through a shared blackboard.

Working prototype / In active development

RIS-Schadenersatzrecht-1

A benchmark testing whether AI models can identify the passages in a supplied Austrian Supreme Court (OGH) decision that answer a legal question. It evaluates the completeness and precision of the selected evidence.

v1.5 / Pending expert review

Fundstelle

Converts legal journal PDFs into Markdown with original page numbers, linked footnotes and bibliographic metadata. Processing takes place locally in the browser.

Working prototype / In active development

JLit-MD

A proposed Markdown format for legal scholarship, with bibliographic metadata, original page references and linked footnotes. Validation gates check structural consistency and bind the text to a specific source version.

Working prototype / In active development

Literaturmonitor

Brings together new Austrian legal publications in a searchable overview, with filters by area of law and publication type. Provides bibliographic details and formatted citations ready to copy.

Early prototype

Zitationsmonitor

Tracks citations to scholarly publications in decisions of the Austrian Supreme Court (OGH). Matches references against a publication list and links each result to the relevant passage in the decision.

In active development

Project LUCID

An experimental review workflow for legal scholarship that flags possible contradictions, inconsistent criteria and gaps in an argument. Findings are tied to specific passages for review by the author.

In active development
